in court. An arbitrator decides the dispute instead of a judge or jury. There is less discovery. Arbitration takes place
on an individual basis. Class proceedings are not permitted. I am waiving the right to trial by jury or to participate in a
class or representative action.

“Claim” means any claim or dispute between Us.
“Covered Claim” means a Claim that must be arbitrated.

“FAA” means the Federal Arbitration Act.
"I", "me" and “my” mean a person who signs the Loan Agreement as a borrower, co-signer or non-obligor.
“Loan” means the loan described in the Loan Agreement.

"Us" or “We” means You and I.
“You" and “your” mean the lender named in the Loan Agreement, its predecessors, and its past, present, and future subsidiaries,

2. WHAT CLAIMS ARE COVERED? You or I may elect to arbitrate any Covered Claim. The term “Covered Claim” is intended to be
interpreted broadly. It consists of any Claim arising out of or related to the subjects below.
Any aspect of the relationship between You and Me.
The Account, the Loan, and any related loan made or serviced by You.
The Agreement, any other Loan document related to the Loan or a related loan made or serviced by You.
Any promotions, advertising, disclosures, negotiations, closing, servicing, collections and enforcement activity.
Any allegations of fraud, misrepresentation, or breach of the Agreement.
Whether the Agreement or any other Loan document is valid or can be enforced.
Any act or omission by You or Me.
Any communications by or on behalf of You, such as calls, faxes, emails or text messages.
The collection, retention, protection, use or disclosure of information about Me or the Account.
A Covered Claim must be arbitrated even if it arose before this or any prior Agreement. This Arbitration Agreement survives termination of
any loan or contract between Us. For example, if the Account or Loan has been cancelled, modified, sold, assigned, refinanced, paid in full,
charged off or discharged or modified in bankruptcy, this Arbitration Agreement still applies.
The FAA governs this Arbitration Agreement.
3. COVERED CLAIMS BY OR AGAINST THIRD PARTIES. A Covered Claim must be arbitrated even if brought by or against the third
parties listed below.
Any users or beneficiaries of the Account or Loan and My and their assignees, heirs, trustees, and agents.
Your employees, officers, agents and service providers.
Any entity providing insurance in connection with any transaction between Us.
Any entity providing any product or service I purchased with Your involvement.
Any entity involved in collection or repossession efforts.
4. WHAT CLAIMS ARE NOT COVERED? The right to use legal self-help or garnishment, repossession or replevin is not subject to
arbitration. I may assert any defenses to those remedies in court. But any counterclaim must be arbitrated if anyone elects arbitration. In
addition, You or I may bring an individualized action in small claims court. If it is removed or appealed, either party can elect arbitration. A
court must decide disputes over whether a Claim can or must be arbitrated or whether this Arbitration Agreement is enforceable. Courts
also decide whether Sections 6, 11, or 13 of this Arbitration Agreement have been violated. Finally, anyone may bring issues to the attention
of federal, state or local agencies or law enforcement. Those agencies can seek relief against You on My behalf.
5. NOTICE OF DISPUTE AND INFORMAL RESOLUTION. Before starting arbitration, You or I must first send the other a written Notice of
Dispute (“Notice”). The Notice to You must be sent to OneMain Financial, Legal Department, 100 International Drive, 16th Floor, Baltimore,
MD 21202 (“Notice Address”). The Notice to Me must be sent to the address on file with my Account. The Notice must be signed, include My
name and Account number, and describe the dispute and relief sought. If I have retained an attorney, I also must include a signed statement
authorizing You to disclose My records to My attorney.
After a complete Notice is received, either party can request an informal settlement phone or videoconference within 60 days. If timely
requested, the conference will take place at a mutually agreeable time. I and a Lender representative must both personally participate in a
good-faith effort to settle the dispute without arbitration. Any counsel representing Us also may participate. The requirement of personal
participation may be waived only if both of Us agree in writing.
Any statute of limitations for the claims and relief set forth in a Notice is tolled during the “Informal Resolution Period.” That Period starts
when a Notice is received. And it ends 60 days later, unless an Informal Settlement Conference was timely requested. If so, the Period ends
seven days after the request is withdrawn or the Conference is completed.
6. COMMENCING ARBITRATION. Arbitration may be started only if the requirements of Section 5 have been fully met. (If your Notice is part
of a mass arbitration, Section 10 has more requirements.) A court can enforce those requirements. That includes the power to enjoin the
filing or prosecution of arbitrations. Unless prohibited by applicable law, the arbitration provider shall not administer any arbitration unless
these requirements have been met.
7. ARBITRATION FORUM AND RULES. The arbitration will be administered by the American Arbitration Association (“AAA”). The AAA will
apply the Consumer Arbitration Rules (“AAA Rules”), as modified by this Arbitration Agreement. If the AAA cannot or will not administer
arbitrations consistent with this Arbitration Agreement or you object to the AAA for good cause, You and I will select another arbitration
provider. If We cannot agree on a provider, the court will choose one. The AAA Rules are available online at https://www.adr.org/consumer.
I can also write to the Notice Address for a copy.
THIS AGREEMENT IS SUBJECT TO THE FEDERAL ARBITRATION ACT.

8. SELECTION OF ARBITRATOR. The arbitration provider will list seven candidate arbitrators. You and I will take turns striking strike three
each from that list. I get to go first. The candidate remaining will be the arbitrator. The arbitrator must be a lawyer with at least 10 years’
experience or a retired judge.
9. COSTS OF ARBITRATION. The arbitration provider charges fees. I may have to pay some fees. If I cannot pay my share or think it is too
high, You will consider any reasonable request to pay those fees, so long as I have complied with Sections 5, 6, 11 and 13. You will also
bear any costs You are required to bear by law or by the terms of any other agreement with Me.
10. CONDUCT OF PROCEEDINGS. All issues are for the arbitrator to decide, except as specified in this Arbitration Agreement. The
arbitrator must honor all recognized privileges (such as the attorney-client privilege and attorney work product doctrine). The arbitrator may
consider rulings in other arbitrations involving other claimants. But an arbitrator’s ruling will not be binding in proceedings involving different
claimants. Except as specified in Section 13 below, the arbitrator can award the same relief that a court can award under applicable law. I
may also be paid $1,000 instead of a smaller award (the “Minimum Recovery”). There are three requirements to be eligible. First, I cannot
have told the arbitrator the amount of any settlement offer. Second, I must have complied with the Notice and Informal Settlement
Conference requirements. Third, the arbitrator must have awarded Me more than the last written settlement offer to Me before the arbitrator
was selected. In making that calculation, amounts offered or awarded for attorneys’ fees and expenses should be excluded. The arbitrator
may decide if the Minimum Recovery should be paid if asked within 14 days of the ruling on the merits.
11. MASS FILINGS. This Section 11 applies if 25 or more claimants submit similar Notices or file similar arbitrations and are represented by
the same or coordinated counsel. If so, all of the cases must be resolved in arbitration in stages using staged bellwether proceedings. I
agree to do this even though My arbitration might be delayed. In the first stage, each side shall select up to 10 cases. Those cases are filed
in arbitration and resolved by separate arbitrators. In the meantime, no other cases may be filed in arbitration. Nor may the arbitration
provider accept, administer or demand payment for fees for other arbitrations. After the first stage of cases are done, You will pay for a
single mediation of all remaining cases. If the remaining cases are not settled, We will repeat the process of filing up to 20 cases, arbitrating
them, and then mediation. If any cases still remain, the process will be repeated until all claims are resolved, with two differences. First, 50
cases can be filed in arbitration each round. Second, mediation is optional at the agreement of both sides. If Section 11 applies to a Notice,
the Informal Resolution Period will be extended until that Notice is selected for a bellwether proceeding or otherwise resolved. A court will
have the authority to enforce this Section. In doing so, the court may enjoin the filing or prosecution of arbitrations or assessment of related
fees. If a court decides that this Section is unenforceable, then it will be severed, but the Minimum Recovery will be unavailable.
12. ENFORCEMENT AND APPEAL OF DECISION. The arbitrator’s ruling is binding. It may be enforced under the FAA in any court with
jurisdiction. If a Covered Claim seeks or results in an award of $100,000 or more (including costs and attorneys’ fees), any party may appeal
within 30 days to a three-arbitrator panel. The appealing party will pay the costs for the appeal, except as provided by law. That panel will
reconsider the case de novo. The panel’s decision shall be binding, subject to judicial review under the FAA.
13. REQUIREMENT OF INDIVIDUAL ARBITRATION. The arbitration may award relief (including injunctions) only in favor of the individual
party seeking relief. The arbitrator may not award relief affecting third parties’ accounts. We agree that each may bring claims against the
other only in Our individual capacities. Neither of Us may be a plaintiff or class member in any purported class, representative or private
attorney general proceeding. Further, unless everyone agrees, the arbitrator cannot consolidate more than one person’s claims. Nor may the
arbitrator preside over any form of a class, representative or private attorney general proceeding. If any of these prohibitions is
unenforceable as to a particular claim or with respect to a particular request for relief (such as a request for an injunction), then that claim or
request for relief will be decided by a court after all other claims and requests for relief are arbitrated.
